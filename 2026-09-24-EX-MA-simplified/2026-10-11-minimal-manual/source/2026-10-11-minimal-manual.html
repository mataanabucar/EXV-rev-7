Before you begin

About this manual

This manual builds the minimal EX-MA: the same arm, turret, levers and rope circuits as the full design, but with no 2″ PVC conduit. The pedestal stands right against the sandbox wall on two OSB spacer frames, and four 8-32 rods clamp the box, the wall, the spacers and the pedestal together. Everything is sized for the boards you have. Work through the stages in order.

How the pages work

  • Stage header: the stage number and what it achieves.
  • You need: every part and fastener for that stage, with its ID (P05 printed, W01 wood, H03 hardware, S04 stock) and quantity. Lay them out first.
  • Numbered steps: do them in order; each step is one action.
  • Figures: numbered balloons 1 match the list under the figure. Orange arrows show where a part moves to.
  • Check boxes: stop and verify before going on.

Units

Everything you cut or drill is given in inches, to the nearest 1/16″: wood, rods, rope, PTFE tube, PEX and copper, and every hole position. Small grey millimetres follow where the CAD value helps. Printed-part details (M3 screws, Ø60 drums, “3 mm below”) stay metric, as in the project files.

Directions used everywhere

  • +u points away from you at the control box, toward the sandbox (“forward”).
  • +v points to your left; −v to your right. z points up.

Safety and good practice

The EX-MA is hand-powered: no motors, springs or electrics. The hazards are the tools you build it with, cut wire rope, small parts, and pinch points between moving printed parts.

Cut wire rope is sharp. Each cut end of 7×19 rope splays into fine, stiff wires that puncture skin. Wrap tape round the rope before cutting and cut through the tape; wear gloves and eye protection.
Small parts. The 6 mm BBs, M3 and 8-32 nuts and crimp sleeves are choking hazards. Keep the build area and the finished model away from small children.
Pinch points. The slewing ring, the lever hubs and every joint drum can trap fingers while ropes are tensioned. Keep fingers out of the box and the pedestal while someone moves a lever or the wheel.
Saws, jigsaws and drills. Clamp OSB and particle board before cutting or drilling; support both sides of a cut so the blade does not bind. OSB and particle-board dust is fine wood dust with resin: cut outdoors or with dust extraction, and wear a dust mask.
Adhesives. CA glue bonds skin in seconds. Use it with ventilation. Follow the maker’s cure times for wood glue, CA, epoxy and silicone; the spacer seal needs its silicone cured before Stage 7.
Heat. Printed PLA+ softens well below the temperature of a car interior in summer. Do not leave the model in direct sun or in a hot vehicle. Assumption
Do not force a joint. Every lever has a hard stop at the end of its slot in the box top. If a lever or the wheel resists before reaching a stop, find the cause (page “Troubleshooting”) instead of pushing harder.
Sand and particle board. Particle board swells and crumbles if it gets wet. Seal the floor (Stage 3), keep the sand dry and keep it below the pedestal top. Assumption

Workspace

  • A flat table about 4 × 2-1/2 ft; good light (rope holes are Ø2.4 mm).
  • Saw horses or a bench for breaking down the 2 × 4 ft OSB panels; a straight edge or a fence for the rip cuts.
  • Small trays or bags for hardware: one per stage. Masking tape and a fine marker for labelling every piece.

How it works

Every circuit is a pull-pull loop: one rope, anchored on two drums. Turning the control drum pulls one strand and lets out the other by the same amount, so the joint drum, and the joint, turns. Nothing else is connected to that rope, so each control moves only its own joint.

Fig. 2Rope circuits (the “wiring diagram” of this machine). Each lever drives a Ø60 drum on the common lever axle; the slew wheel drives a Ø110 two-lane spool. Lengths are the cut lengths for the minimal build.
Fig. 3Every rope as modelled, from its control to its joint (cut through the middle; built pose).

The spacer chamber replaces the conduit

Between the box fitting and the pedestal fitting the ropes and tubes cross a 3-1/8″ chamber: the holes in the box front, the sandbox wall and the pedestal wall, and the windows in the two spacer frames, sealed on all sides. The stick and bucket tubes keep their spare length there as a gentle bow (radius about 1-3/16″, more than the 5/8″ minimum). Derived

Why the stick and bucket ropes run in PTFE tubes

Their ropes cross the boom joint (the bucket ropes the stick joint too). Inside a tube anchored at the box fitting and at a stop in the arm, the rope length does not change when another joint moves. The tubes cross each joint through the pivot axis (the pins are split in two for this). The slew drum sits in the pedestal on the 3/4″ PEX turret tube. Verified

What is different in the minimal build Derived

  • No conduit. The pedestal stands against the sandbox wall on two glued-on OSB spacer frames (W22, W23). Four 8-32 rods run from the box fitting (P18) to the pedestal fitting (P17) and clamp the whole stack; they replace the 8 M3 fitting bolts.
  • Your boards. 7/16″ OSB for every panel except the sandbox floor, which is the 5/8″ particle board used whole. With no shim, the sandbox and pedestal walls are cut 5/32″ shorter so the turret and fittings stay at their design heights, and the elbow support loses its base plate.
  • Compact box with your prints. 8-7/16″ deep, 6-1/8″ high, 15″ wide (old hubs at 2-3/4″ spacing, old spool); handles 5-11/16″; the slew wheel sits 3-3/4″ above the lid.
  • Order. The rods go into the pedestal in Stage 4; the tubes are seated in P18 and the box goes onto the rods in Stage 7. The lever axle goes in before the left box side; the joint end of each arm rope is rigged while its arm half is open.

After assembly

Operation and maintenance

Operating

  • Stand behind the box. Left hand: boom and stick levers. Right hand: bucket lever and slew wheel.
  • Move one control at a time at first. Push forward = boom down, stick out, bucket dump; wheel left = arm left.
  • The slot ends are the stops. Do not lever against them or force the wheel past ±90°.
  • Do not lift or carry the machine by the arm, the levers or the wheel. Carry the sandbox and the box together from below.
  • Dig in dry sand only. Brush sand off the slewing ring and out of the lever slots after use.

Open items and verification

Found by checking the project’s CAD and hardware model. Items 1–6 and 8 are in the project files as they are; item 7 comes from the minimal build with your prints. Each needs your decision. Open item

#What the files showOptions
1Stick-drum key screws collide
The stick drum is 10 mm thick and each key pilot runs right through it. The hardware model puts an M3 × 12 into each pilot from each side; the two tips overlap by 1.6 mm.		Use M3 × 8 socket caps on the stick drum (in your kit), or grind 2 mm off each M3 × 12.
2Stick root lugs overlap
Joining lugs 1 and 2 of the stick are 2.9 mm apart, so their holes merge and their screws and nuts overlap.		Fit one M3 × 20 in that lug; the stick then has 3 lug screws (1 spare).
3Joining-lug screws are enclosed
On both members every lug screw head sits inside the closed shell, and the outer walls have no access hole on the screw axis.		Decide before Stage 8: (a) drill a small access hole on each lug axis; (b) close the halves without lug screws, held with tape or dots of CA along the seam; (c) add access in the CAD and reprint the halves.
45/16″ axle ends
Both axles carry 5/16″ nuts. One design note calls the rod “smooth”.		Threaded rod: nothing changes. Smooth rod: thread the ends with a 5/16-18 die or use 5/16″ shaft collars.
5Pedestal not screwed to the floor
The cut list says the pedestal screws to the sandbox floor, but no screws are counted. In the minimal build the four rods clamp it to the sandbox wall.		Optional: 4 extra #6 × 1-1/4″ up through the floor into the ±v walls (Stage 4).
6Tension direction
The checklist says to slide the rope anchors “outward”. In the CAD the rope enters each slot at its outer end, so the strand tightens as the screw moves inward.		Slide toward the drum centre (Stages 9 and 10).
7Bucket knob near the slew wheel
With the kept 145 mm spool the wheel sits 3-3/4″ above the lid. At the bucket lever’s end of travel its knob comes within 15.5 mm (5/8″) of the wheel rim; the full design had 24 mm.		Works as is; mind your knuckles on the bucket lever. A reprinted short spool (compact spec) would lower the wheel.
8Slicer plates hold the old base and ears
Plates 4 and 6 of the PLA+ project were made before the 2026-09-29 rebuilds.		If you have not printed P09, P10 and P12 yet, use the 2026-09-29 STL files (Stage 1).
